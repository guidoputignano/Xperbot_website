The problem

The bottleneck is not the robot.
It is the conversation.

Robotics has an expression problem. The industry expert knows exactly what the job requires but cannot state it in machine terms. The robot supplier has the capability but cannot see the scene. Between them sits a slow, expensive, custom-engineering process that most projects never survive.

No standard way to describe a scene

Vertical scenarios have no shared vocabulary, so every deployment restarts custom development from zero — high cost, long cycles.

Suppliers misread the requirement

Robot companies struggle to interpret vertical needs precisely, so scene adaptation is slow and the first pilot often solves the wrong problem.

No common language between the two

Domain experts and robotics engineers talk past each other. Procurement decisions get made on brochures instead of evidence.

How it works

Four steps, one guided flow.

No robotics vocabulary required at any point. The platform does the translation.

01

Guided scene definition

Describe the job the way you would to a colleague — in natural language, or by picking an industry template and dragging task nodes into place. Choose the scene closest to your deployment, the tasks the robot must handle, the traits of your environment and the capabilities you need.

02

Intelligent parsing and matching

The platform decomposes your description into structured requirements — environment parameters such as aisle width, then functional needs such as autonomous obstacle avoidance — and maps them onto robot skill combinations. A knowledge graph and scoring engine rank the whole model database against your scene.

03

Digital-twin validation

Preview the task chain in 3D before you commit. Adjust parameters live, watch where the workflow breaks, and get an honest suitability score instead of a sales promise.

04

Export a deployment-ready solution

One click produces a Robot Deployment Assessment Report — recommended models with specifications, requirements analysis, safety assessment, compliance assessment and a phased implementation roadmap — plus a standardised requirements document and API output for your supplier.

Who it is for

Three sides of the same gap.

Domain experts

You know the job, not the robot

Clinicians, logistics managers, facility operators. You understand the pain point in detail but have no way to convert it into parameters an engineer can build against. RSIP does that conversion for you.

Enterprise buyers

You need proof before you purchase

Factories, warehouses, service operators wanting to cut cost and raise throughput — but facing hard selection decisions and expensive trial-and-error. Validate feasibility before the capital request.

Technology suppliers

You have capability, not reach

Algorithm firms and hardware makers with strong technology that cannot efficiently find the niches where it fits. Reach real, specified scenes and cut the cost of custom development.

Business model

A two-sided platform.

Scene owners subscribe to find and validate solutions. Technology providers pay for qualified, already-specified demand. Both sides make the other more valuable.

Scene owners pay for

Finding and proving the right solution

  • PaaS subscription — guided scene definition, digital twin, solution proposals
  • Quotation and plausibility assessment
  • Advanced procurement consulting
  • Configuration, application training and continuous update service
Technology providers pay for

Reaching specified demand

  • PaaS subscription and transaction fees
  • RaaS revenue share on deployed fleets
  • API calls and technology licensing
  • Lead generation, promotion and physical-AI training tools
